01Strategy case
Market selection for a hypothetical Kenyan mobile-money operator

Africa's mobile-money frontier: where to expand next, and what you would need to believe

Kenya's home market is close to full. We scored 25 sub-Saharan markets on attractiveness and ease of entry, built business cases for the top five, and tested what would have to be true for each to pay back.

Data: Global Findex 2025 (survey 2024), World Development Indicators, World Bank FY26 fragility listIndependent analysis, October 2026
02Answer first

Enter Tanzania first, hold DR Congo as a gated option, and do not commit to a second market until the Tanzania gate is passed

87.5%of Kenyan adults use mobile money: little room left at home
#1 Tanzaniatop priority score (66/100) and the only large market that is also easy
$13.5mTanzania Base-case NPV; range −$102m to $194m
13.6%share of new users Tanzania needs to break even, against 15% assumed
Why Tanzania

Highest priority score, a Base revenue pool of $673m in 2030, adoption of 53% against Kenya's 87%, and no fragility flag. It is the only market where the Base case is positive and the downside is survivable.

Why not a flat "enter two markets"

Only Tanzania and DR Congo are positive in the Base case, and both only just. Cote d'Ivoire and Uganda need more than the assumed share to break even. Fundamentals also fail to predict who wins locally, so each entry needs a diligence gate.

The ask: approve a gated Tanzania entry with a peak funding need of about $56m, and a diligence study on DR Congo and Cote d'Ivoire to decide the second move by the 2028 gate.

03Context

Kenya's home market is near its ceiling at 87% adoption, while most of the region is at half that or less

Share of adults with a mobile money account, Global Findex survey rounds.

Growth at home has to slow

Kenya's adult population is 34.6 million and 87% already use mobile money. Further growth is mostly revenue per user, not new users.

The runway is elsewhere

Tanzania, Cote d'Ivoire and Uganda sit at 53%, 53% and 68%. DR Congo and Nigeria are at 35% and 33% with far larger populations.

The question for the board

Which two markets, in what order, and on what evidence? The next slides answer that.

05Adoption

Adoption has risen fast but unevenly, and the Base forecast has most large markets still below Kenya's 87% in 2030

20172024 survey2030 Base forecast
Method

Logistic (S-curve) diffusion: each market's log-odds of adoption grows at a common 0.18 a year, with the Low and High scenarios at half and one and a half times that pace, capped at 92%.

Why a common rate

A back-test from the 2022 round showed it beat country-specific trends: 7.4 against 10.8 points of average error.

Reading

Ten markets with the largest winnable revenue in 2030 (Base).

06Forecast credibility

A single regional growth rate forecast 2024 adoption better than country-specific trends, but ran 2.8 points high, so the Low case matters

Forecast error with this weight on the country's own trend (points)Error if adoption is assumed not to grow

Horizontal axis: weight given to each country's own trend. 34 countries; forecasts made from the 2022 round for 2024.

What it tells us

Country-specific trends add noise: error rises from 7.4 to 10.8 points as they get more weight. We therefore use a common rate.

What it cannot do

The forecast was 2.8 points too high on average. The Low scenario (half the growth) is the prudent planning case, and it is where Tanzania's NPV is −$102m.

10Robustness

Tanzania and DR Congo are the most frequent top-two markets across 5,000 random weightings; beyond them the ranking is unstable, so we recommend a sequence

Share of 5,000 draws, with all attractiveness and ease weights re-drawn at random (Dirichlet), in which the market ranks in the top two.

What is robust

Tanzania ranks first in 26% of draws and DR Congo is the next most frequent top-two market (40%).

What is not

Positions three to ten swap easily. Liberia appears in the top two in 31% of draws on ease alone, but its 2030 pool is only $41m and it covers 0.2× its fixed cost, so the business-case screen would remove it.

Implication

Use the score to shortlist, then decide on the business case and on diligence evidence.

Break-even share of new users

A well-funded challenger takes roughly one in seven new mobile-money users, at Kenyan-style revenue per user adjusted for Tanzanian income. A 20% shortfall in revenue per user needs about 15% more share to still break even.

Appendix B. Method and integrity checks

Method in six lines

  1. Screen: Kenya excluded, markets under 3m adults dropped (25 candidates).
  2. Forecast: logit-space diffusion, common growth 0.179 a year, ceiling 92%; Low, Base and High scenarios.
  3. Pools: users × revenue per user; revenue per user adjusted for income and mobile-led structure.

18Appendix C

Appendix C. Sources and limitations

Sources

  1. S1 Global Findex Database 2025 (survey year 2024; earlier rounds 2011-2021/22) (World Bank / Gates Foundation / Gallup). Used for: Mobile money, account and merchant-payment adoption; gender, income and rural gaps; adult population
  2. S2 World Development Indicators (DataBank extract, last updated 8 Oct 2026) (World Bank). Used for: GDP per capita, urbanisation, mobile subscriptions, internet, electricity, remittances, inflation, exchange rate, population growth
  3. S3 Safaricom FY2026 results (year to March 2026), M-Pesa revenue and active customers (Safaricom PLC, as reported by HapaKenya). Used for: Calibration of revenue per active user in the home market
  4. S4 Airtel Africa FY2026 final results: Airtel Money revenue and customers by region (Airtel Africa plc). Used for: Calibration of revenue per customer in East Africa and Nigeria; low-revenue bank-led markets
  5. S5 Harmonized List of Fragile and Conflict-Affected Situations, FY26 (World Bank). Used for: Institutional stability score (conflict = 0, institutional/social fragility = 40, not listed = 100)

Limitations we are open about

  1. Governance indicators (WGI: government effectiveness GE.EST, regulatory quality RQ.EST) were not in the data supplied. Institutional stability is therefore proxied by the World Bank FY26 fragility list, which is coarse (three levels). Adding WGI is the first extension.
  2. No Findex codebook was available. Variable meanings were taken from names and checked against published headline figures (for example Kenya's mobile-money adoption).
  3. Rwanda, Burundi, Somalia and South Sudan have no 2024 Findex mobile-money result, so they cannot be scored.
  4. Findex measures the share of adults reporting a mobile money account. Operator-reported 'active users' are defined differently and are not comparable one-to-one.
  5. Revenue per user is calibrated on two company disclosures reported in the press (S3, S4), not audited filings. It is scaled to other markets by income and by how mobile-led the market is. The Low/High range spans the uncertainty.
  6. Judgement inputs, flagged [A] in the workbook: proximity to Kenya, operator share of new and existing users, margin, fixed cost, launch investment, terminal multiple and discount-rate premia. Each is tested in the Sensitivity sheet or through the break-even share.
  7. A cross-country regression of adoption on economic fundamentals does not predict adoption out-of-sample (leave-one-country-out error 25.5 points against 21.0 for a no-fundamentals benchmark). The regression is therefore reported, not used in scoring.
  8. The adoption forecast over-predicted 2024 levels by 2.8 points on average in a back-test from 2022. The Low scenario halves the trend to cover this.
  9. Ranks beyond the top two are sensitive to the scoring weights (see Robustness). The recommendation rests on the top of the ranking and on the gate sequence, not on a precise ordering.
  10. Business-case values are an illustrative, entry-level screen (no tax, no working capital, no currency path beyond the discount-rate premium). They are not a valuation.
